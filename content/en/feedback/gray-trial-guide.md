# Trial Reading And Feedback Guide

Thank you for reading and reviewing the English web edition of Health Youpu. This edition is a U.S.-first adaptation based on the Chinese v1.0.0 structure. It can be read continuously or used by situation, and it is meant for reviewing reading flow, usefulness, and medical boundaries. It is not meant to be shared as medical advice.

## Start With The Boundary

This book does not provide diagnosis, medication advice, stopping-medication advice, screening advice, or treatment advice, and it cannot replace a clinician. If chest pain, stroke-like symptoms, severe trouble breathing, fainting, altered consciousness, severe pain, unusual bleeding, self-harm or suicide risk, or other red flags appear, contact local emergency services or seek medical care promptly.

If you are facing a specific health problem while reading, use clinicians, emergency care, local triage lines, or credible medical institutions first. This book only helps you organize the question, sort urgency, prepare communication, and recognize information traps.

## Recommended Reading Paths

You do not need to read the whole book from the beginning. Choose the path closest to your current situation.

| What you want to do now | Open first |
|---|---|
| Someone already feels unwell, and you are unsure whether it can wait | [Red Flags](../handbook/playbooks/red-flags.md); if it is not an obvious emergency, use [Symptom Action Guide](../handbook/playbooks/symptom-action-guide.md) |
| You need to review a report, follow up, or see a clinician | [Checkup Markers](../book/part-1-healthspan-risk-and-markers/checkup-markers.md) or [Doctor Visit Checklist](../handbook/playbooks/doctor-visit-checklist.md) |
| You want to prepare for family care | [One-Page Family Health Card](../handbook/templates/family-health-record.md#_1-one-page-family-health-card), then [Family Health Record And Chronic Marker Log](../handbook/templates/family-health-record.md) |
| You want to read systematically | [Start Here](../book/00-start-here.md), then [Book Contents](../book/README.md) |
| Supplements, tests, devices, or longevity products are causing family debate | [Health Product Checklist](../handbook/templates/health-product-checklist.md), then [Supplement Evidence Traps](../book/part-4-health-information-defense/supplement-evidence-traps.md) |

If you only have 20 minutes, read:

1. [Start Here](../book/00-start-here.md)
2. One chapter or tool page closest to your real life;
3. Then answer: was the entry smooth, was the next step clear, what still sounds like filler, and what does not yet feel like a real book?

## Reading One Page Is Enough

Many useful comments do not require reading the whole book. Pick one page and write one honest reaction while reading.

| Time available | How to read | Most useful feedback |
|---|---|---|
| 5 minutes | Open one page and scan headings and tables | Whether the page's job is obvious at a glance |
| 10 minutes | Read one tool page, such as symptom sorting or the family health card | Whether you know what to do next afterward |
| 20 minutes | Read one chapter and one related tool page | Whether the chapter and tool connect naturally |
| 30 minutes | Run a real family scenario through the flow | Where you get stuck, what is missing, and what feels unsafe to act on |

## What To Look For

Please try not to only say whether the writing is good. These questions are more useful:

1. After opening a page, can you quickly find where to go next?
2. After reading, is the difference between recording, watching, follow-up, clinical care, and emergency care clearer?
3. Which parts feel generic, correct but unhelpful, or still leave you unsure what to do?
4. Which wording feels preachy, frightening, or too much like making a medical decision for the reader?
5. What is truly useful and should become a tool, checklist, or template?
6. If you read a full chapter, does it feel like someone helping you think, or like looking up notes?

## What Useful Feedback Looks Like

It does not need to be a formal review. Short comments like these help most:

| Feedback type | Example |
|---|---|
| Filler | "This paragraph sounds right, but I do not know what to do with it." |
| Unclear boundary | "This puts chest tightness and chest pain together; I am not sure whether it could mislead people." |
| Missing situation | "Does this still work if the older adult lives with adult children instead of alone?" |
| Hard to keep reading | "The first three paragraphs are too long on mobile." |
| Unclear term | "I do not know what CBT-I means here." |
| Useful | "I would send this table to my family because it does not sound like it is lecturing them." |

If you can only write one sentence, write:

```text
My honest reaction at this point is:
```

## How To Send Feedback

Use the project's GitHub repository or the maintainer channel you already use. If you send a note directly, include "Health Youpu English feedback" so it can be sorted into the next maintenance pass.

## The Three Things The Maintainer Most Wants To Know

- Is the entry smooth: can you reach the right content in one or two clicks?
- Do the tools actually work: are symptom sorting, checkup tools, visit preparation, the family health card, and the health product checklist concrete enough?
- Is the main book worth reading: does it help build steadier health judgment, or does it still feel like source notes? For brain and mental health, children and adolescents, cancer, and specialty-care chapters, does it stay readable without crossing medical boundaries?
