# Family Health Record And Chronic Marker Log

> This template is not medical advice and is not a legal medical record. It helps families keep key facts findable. Health information is private; get consent from the person whenever possible and store it carefully.

This page does only three jobs:

1. a one-page family health card;
2. a chronic marker log;
3. a pre-visit summary.

Do not fill everything at once. If the next visit is less chaotic, the record is already useful.

## 1. One-Page Family Health Card

Use one page per person. Start with the facts most likely to matter during urgent care, follow-up, caregiving, or a hospital discharge. Keep it in a phone note, shared family folder, patient-portal folder, or printed binder front page.

```text
Name:
Birth year:
Primary city/state:
Emergency contact:

Major diagnoses or long-running issues:
Medication allergies or serious reactions:
Current long-term medications:
Major surgeries, hospitalizations, or important tests:
Usual clinic / care team / specialist / clinician:
Insurance, Medicare/Medicaid, or important document location:

Most recent important follow-up:
Next follow-up:
```

If the page feels too large, fill only three fields first: major diagnoses, allergies or serious reactions, and long-term medications. In a rushed moment, those three are often the easiest to miss and the most useful to have.

## 2. Chronic Marker Log

Use this for long-running follow-up situations such as high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol, abnormal uric acid, kidney-function concerns, cardiovascular disease, cancer follow-up, mental health concerns, or repeated symptoms.

Do not collect screenshots forever. For each event, write one entry:

```text
Date:
Setting: home measurement / preventive visit or lab follow-up / clinic visit / symptom change
Marker or issue:
Result:
Unit and reference range:
Compared with last time:
Context: poor sleep / alcohol / infection / pain / stress / exercise / other
Medication, missed-dose, or supplement change:
Symptom or daily function change:
Clinician's next step:
```

Before a follow-up visit, look at three things:

    <div class="decision-label">Trend</div>
    <h3>Is it changing repeatedly?</h3>
    <p>Is this the first abnormal result, or has the same marker been abnormal several times? Is it moving slowly, or did it change suddenly?</p>
  </section>
  <section class="decision-card decision-card-blue">
    <div class="decision-label">Combination</div>
    <h3>Are several things worsening together?</h3>
    <p>Are blood pressure, cholesterol and triglycerides, glucose/A1C, uric acid, kidney function, weight, or waist circumference changing in the same direction?</p>
  </section>
  <section class="decision-card decision-card-yellow">
    <div class="decision-label">Context</div>
    <h3>What happened around the change?</h3>
    <p>Was there a missed dose, poor sleep, alcohol, infection, pain, stress, a medication change, or a new supplement?</p>
  </section>
</div>

Chest pain or pressure, severe trouble breathing, fainting, altered consciousness, stroke-like symptoms, severe hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia symptoms should not wait for a log. Get help first.

## 3. Pre-Visit Summary

Ten minutes before a visit, compress the recent story onto one page. You can copy this:

```text
The main reason for this visit:

Most important change in the past 1-3 months:

Most important markers or reports to review:

What was happening around the abnormal result:

New symptoms or daily function changes:

Medication, missed-dose, stopping, or supplement changes:

Was the last plan completed:

Top 3 questions for the clinician:

Materials brought: reports / home measurements / medication bottles or list / imaging / other
```

The summary is not meant to diagnose. It helps the visit reach the important facts faster. When preparing questions, use it with the [Doctor Visit Checklist](../playbooks/doctor-visit-checklist.md).

## Storage Rules

Keep paper copies in a predictable place that the actual caregiver knows. Keep electronic copies somewhere reliable. Do not post full private information in a large family chat.

For adults, medical, medication, and mental health information should generally be controlled by the person whenever possible. For older adults, children, or people who need caregiving support, share only what actual care and medical coordination require.

## One Sentence

A family health record is not a way to turn life into paperwork; it is a way to avoid ten rounds of "where is that thing?" when it matters.
