# Family Health Record

> This template is not medical advice and is not a legal medical record. It helps families keep key facts findable. Health information is private; get consent from the person whenever possible and store it carefully.

[中文验收页](../../../en-review/zh-CN/handbook/templates/family-health-record.md)

**Location:** Handbook / Templates

Use this when a one-page card is not enough. Do not build a perfect archive first. Start with the facts that make the next visit, urgent-care trip, hospital discharge, or caregiving handoff less chaotic.

## One-Page Profile

```text
Name:
Date of birth:
Primary city/state:
Preferred language:
Emergency contact:
Primary care clinician / clinic:
Pharmacy:

Major diagnoses or long-running issues:
Medication allergies or serious reactions:
Current medicines and supplements:
Major surgeries, hospitalizations, or procedures:
Important devices or implants:
Insurance / Medicare / Medicaid / important document location:

Recent major visit or hospitalization:
Next follow-up:
Patient portal access / record location:
```

If this feels too much, fill only three fields first: major diagnoses, allergies/reactions, and current medicines.

## Visit And Follow-Up Log

```text
Date:
Reason for visit:
Clinician / location:
Main assessment:
Tests ordered:
Medication or care changes:
Return precautions:
Results to check:
Follow-up date:
Who is handling the next step:
```

Write facts, not interpretations. "CT done; result expected Friday" is more useful than "probably fine."

## Records To Keep Findable

- medication list, allergies, and pharmacy;
- recent labs and imaging reports;
- discharge instructions and return precautions;
- specialist notes and referral information;
- vaccination record when available;
- advance directive, health care proxy, or emergency contact information if the person has chosen to share it;
- insurance card, Medicare/Medicaid information, and important billing contacts where relevant.

## Privacy Defaults

Do not put full records in a large family chat. Decide who can see what, why they need it, and where the record lives.

For adults, access should come from consent whenever possible. For older adults, caregiving, disability, or emergencies, keep the circle small and practical. For children, protect the record as sensitive family information.
