# Common Checkup Markers: Sort Before You Interpret

> This page is not medical advice. It cannot interpret your personal lab report, diagnose disease, set targets, recommend medication changes, or replace a clinician who knows your symptoms, history, exam, prior results, and medications.

[中文验收页](../../../en-review/zh-CN/handbook/playbooks/common-checkup-markers.md)

This page is not a mini textbook for every lab value. It helps a family do three safer things: sort urgency, place the abnormal result on a risk line, and prepare better questions for a clinician.

## 30-Second Sort

**Red: symptoms outrank the report.**

Chest pain or pressure, stroke-like symptoms, severe shortness of breath, fainting, altered mental status, abnormal bleeding, severe pain, self-harm risk, or rapidly worsening illness should move to emergency or urgent professional help. Do not keep studying numbers.

**Yellow: marked, persistent, or clustered abnormalities.**

Repeated abnormal results, clearly abnormal values, or several metabolic, kidney, liver, blood, thyroid, weight, waist, or symptom changes moving together deserve a prepared clinical conversation.

**Green: one mild, isolated flag.**

Check the reference range, test conditions, recent illness, medications, supplements, alcohol, sleep, exercise, dehydration, pregnancy or postpartum context, and prior trend. Follow the report or clinician's advice about repeat testing. Do not self-diagnose from one arrow.

## Look At Four Things First

- **First time or trend:** a single mild flag may need confirmation; repeated or worsening results matter more.
- **Single or clustered:** blood pressure, cholesterol, glucose or A1C, uric acid, waist size, and kidney markers moving together matter more than one isolated number.
- **Context:** infection, hard exercise, alcohol, sleep loss, stress, pregnancy, recent surgery, medications, supplements, dehydration, and lab method can change results.
- **Symptoms:** a normal report does not explain away chest pain, severe shortness of breath, stroke-like symptoms, severe pain, abnormal bleeding, or mental health crisis risk.

## Common Markers By Risk Line

| What you see | Put it next to | Better next question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High blood pressure reading | Home readings, cuff size, rest before measurement, symptoms, kidney markers, glucose, cholesterol, medications | Is this one reading, a repeated pattern, or a situation that needs prompt care? |
| Cholesterol or triglyceride flag | LDL-C, HDL-C, triglycerides, blood pressure, diabetes risk, smoking, kidney disease, family history | What is the overall cardiovascular risk, not just total cholesterol? |
| Glucose or A1C flag | Fasting or nonfasting state, weight or waist trend, lipids, kidney markers, anemia, pregnancy/postpartum context | Is this a result to confirm, a prevention window, or known diabetes follow-up? |
| High uric acid | Gout symptoms, kidney stones, kidney function, alcohol, sugary drinks, weight, medications | Is the issue production, clearance, gout risk, kidney context, or a mixed pattern? |
| Creatinine, eGFR, urine albumin, protein, or blood in urine | Blood pressure, diabetes risk, medications, hydration, urine collection method, prior kidney results | Does this need a standardized repeat test, UACR, clinician review, or referral? |
| ALT, AST, GGT, bilirubin | Alcohol, fatty liver, medications, supplements, infection, hard exercise, gallbladder symptoms | What background or repeat path should be checked before buying "liver support"? |
| CBC flag | Fever, infection, fatigue, bleeding, periods, bruising, medications, prior CBC | Which blood cell line is abnormal, and is it new, severe, or persistent? |
| TSH, FT4, FT3 flag | Palpitations, heat/cold intolerance, weight change, pregnancy, thyroid medication, thyroid nodules | Does the thyroid result match symptoms and the person's context? |
| Tumor marker flag | Age, family history, symptoms, imaging, endoscopy, prior results, why the test was ordered | What was this marker meant to do, and what diagnostic step is actually next? |
| Nodule, cyst, or polyp on imaging/endoscopy | Location, size, shape, prior images, report recommendation, symptoms | Is this watchful follow-up, specialist review, or further testing? |

## Five Sentences To Bring To A Visit

```text
Is this the first abnormal result, or has it happened before?
What recent illness, alcohol, sleep loss, hard exercise, medications, supplements, pregnancy/postpartum context, or dehydration could matter?
Are there symptoms or daily-function changes that change the urgency?
Which risk line does this belong to?
Is the next step recording, repeat testing, more testing, treatment discussion, referral, or urgent evaluation?
```

## Do Not Do These

- Do not stop, start, or change medication because of a single flag.
- Do not use a normal tumor marker to rule out cancer.
- Do not use a normal report to explain away dangerous symptoms.
- Do not mix results from different labs, units, or test methods as if they were identical.
- Do not turn an internet threshold into your personal treatment target.
- Do not buy supplements before you know what problem the result is pointing to.

Checkup markers are most useful when they help you sort urgency, trend, context, combination, and the next clinical question.
