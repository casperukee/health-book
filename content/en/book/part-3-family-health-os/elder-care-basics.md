# Elder Care Basics: Protect The Ability To Live

> This page is not medical advice. Falls with injury, head injury, sudden confusion, sudden weakness, chest pain, breathing trouble, severe pain, abnormal bleeding, serious medication reaction, or rapid decline need prompt medical help.

[中文验收页](../../../en-review/zh-CN/book/part-3-family-health-os/elder-care-basics.md)

Elder care often starts before anyone says "caregiving." It starts when a parent walks less, stops going out, mixes medications, almost falls, avoids bathing, misses bills, or says "I'm fine" in a way that does not match daily life.

The family question is not "How do we control everything?" It is: what abilities are becoming fragile, and what can we protect early?

## Three Kinds Of Change

| Change | Meaning | Family response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sudden change | fall with head injury, unable to bear weight, confusion, stroke-like symptoms, severe breathlessness, chest pain, sudden vision change | Use urgent/emergency care; do not explain it away as age |
| Gradual change | near-falls, less walking, medication mistakes, weight/appetite change, memory affecting daily tasks, new sleepiness after medication changes | Record examples and arrange medical review |
| No crisis yet | home hazards, unclear medication list, missed follow-up, poor lighting, no emergency contacts | Prepare the home, records, and follow-up routines |

Older adults may show illness as "not themselves," sudden confusion, weakness, less eating, a fall, or loss of function. Treat sudden change seriously.

## Four Abilities To Watch

### Mobility

Ask about falls, near-falls, stairs, turning, getting out of bed, bathing, night bathroom trips, shoes, vision, lighting, rugs, cords, and fear of going out.

After a fall, ask whether they hit the head, lost consciousness, vomited, became confused, had severe headache or seizure, can bear weight, have new weakness or numbness, or take blood thinners or antiplatelet medicines.

### Cognition And Judgment

Record concrete function: medication mistakes, leaving the stove on, getting lost, money or scam vulnerability, missed appointments, language trouble, sudden confusion, or behavior change.

Sudden confusion is not normal aging. Gradual cognitive change that affects daily life also deserves evaluation.

### Medication And Recovery

Keep one list that includes prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, eye drops, creams, injections, supplements, herbs, alcohol use, and recent changes.

After a medication change, watch for dizziness, sleepiness, confusion, appetite change, constipation, diarrhea, bleeding, falls, or worse sleep. Do not stop or change medication without clinician guidance.

### Daily Living And Connection

Watch basic activities: eating, dressing, bathing, toileting, getting up, walking. Watch instrumental activities: cooking, shopping, money, medications, phone use, transportation, housework.

Also watch connection: fewer outings, fewer calls, less interest, more loneliness, caregiver strain, or loss of meaningful roles.

## Caregivers Need A System Too

Caregiving can become one person's full-time mental load. Split tasks: visits and notes, records and portal access, refills, transportation, bills, meals, bathing, home safety, and respite.

If a caregiver is exhausted, sleeping poorly, angry, hopeless, afraid to answer the phone, or worried they might harm themselves or the person they care for, the system is overloaded. Seek help.

## A Monthly 15-Minute Check

```text
Mobility: any falls, near-falls, slower walking, fear of bathing or going out?
Cognition: any cooking, medication, money, route, or judgment mistakes?
Medication: any new, stopped, duplicated, missed, or side-effect-prone medicines?
Life: eating, sleep, toileting, bathing, social contact, mood, caregiver strain?
```

Write facts, not blame.
