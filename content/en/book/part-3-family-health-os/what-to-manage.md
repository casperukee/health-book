# What A Family Health System Actually Manages

> This page is not medical advice. Family records and routines cannot replace diagnosis, emergency care, treatment, medication decisions, screening decisions, or guidance from qualified clinicians.

[中文验收页](../../../en-review/zh-CN/book/part-3-family-health-os/what-to-manage.md)

Many family health problems become harder because every event starts from zero. The report is in a photo album. The medication dose is unclear. The patient portal message was sent to one person. The discharge instructions are somewhere in a bag. The family group chat is full of opinions, but no one can find the facts.

A family health system does not manage people. It manages four things:

1. facts;
2. boundaries;
3. next steps;
4. roles.

## Facts

Facts include symptoms, timing, diagnoses, surgeries, hospitalizations, allergies, medication lists, supplements, recent reports, imaging, discharge instructions, and clinician instructions.

Start small. A one-page [Family Health Card](../../handbook/templates/family-health-record.md#_1-one-page-family-health-card) is better than a perfect archive that never gets built.

## Boundaries

Families should not vote on warning signs. Chest pain, stroke-like symptoms, severe breathing trouble, fainting, major confusion, uncontrolled bleeding, serious injury, severe allergic reaction, poisoning, self-harm risk, suicide risk, pregnancy/postpartum warning signs, or rapid worsening illness should move into emergency or urgent care pathways.

For the quick list, use [Red Flags](../../handbook/playbooks/red-flags.md).

## Next Steps

Write down what the clinician thinks is most important, what test or follow-up was recommended, when results should be checked, when to come back earlier, what instructions changed, and who is responsible for the next step.

"Doctor said it is fine" is not enough. Fine in what way? What was ruled out? What still needs follow-up?

## Roles

Roles reduce panic. Decide who can find records, keep medication and allergy lists updated, accompany visits when needed, help with transportation, bills, insurance, pharmacy refills, or portal access, check whether follow-up happened, and give the primary caregiver a break.

Roles are not control. They keep care from depending on one tired person's memory.

## Useful Defaults

Reports go into one folder or portal note. Medication photos are updated after changes. Emergency contacts are easy to find. Follow-up dates go into a calendar if the person agrees. Expired medicines are removed periodically. After-visit instructions are saved before everyone forgets.

Keep privacy explicit. Health information is sensitive. Decide who can see what, when, and why.

## One Sentence

A family health system is useful when facts are findable, danger boundaries are clear, next steps are written down, and roles are shared.
