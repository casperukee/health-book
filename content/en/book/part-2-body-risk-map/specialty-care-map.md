# Specialty Care Map: Bring Facts, Not Self-Diagnosis

> This page is not medical advice. Sudden vision loss, eye injury, pregnancy or postpartum warning signs, serious injury, severe pain, fever with urinary symptoms, inability to urinate, major confusion, weakness or numbness, self-harm risk, or rapidly worsening symptoms need urgent help.

[中文验收页](../../../en-review/zh-CN/book/part-2-body-risk-map/specialty-care-map.md)

Many specialty problems live in an uncomfortable middle zone. They are not always obvious emergencies, but they are not nothing: vision changes, urinary symptoms, pelvic pain, back pain, dental procedures, skin rashes, postpartum problems, sexual health, hearing, thyroid, digestive symptoms, or chronic pain.

The point of a specialty-care map is not to become your own doctor. It is to bring the right facts to the right doorway.

## Ask Four Questions Before "Which Specialist?"

1. Are there red flags?
2. What function is affected?
3. What is the timeline?
4. What should this visit answer?

If there are red flags, do not let department choice delay urgent care.

## A Symptom Card Beats Ten Guesses

Before a visit, write:

| What to record | Useful detail |
| --- | --- |
| Time | when it started, sudden or gradual, getting better or worse |
| Location | where it is, one side or both, spreading or moving |
| Quality | pressure, sharp, burning, numb, itchy, cramping, throbbing, foreign-body feeling, hard to describe |
| Severity | 0-10, whether it wakes the person, whether it is worsening |
| Triggers and relief | movement, posture, food, urination, bowel movement, sex, period, sleep, stress, medication |
| Function | vision, walking, sleep, work, school, eating, urinating, sex, caregiving, social life |
| Associated signs | fever, bleeding, weakness, numbness, dizziness, shortness of breath, rash, weight change, mood change |
| What you tried | medication, ointment, supplement, device, eye drops, physical therapy, dental product, skin product, and side effects |

This is not self-diagnosis. It saves clinic time for judgment.

## Common Specialty Situations

| Situation | What to clarify |
| --- | --- |
| Eyes and vision | Which eye, sudden or gradual change, floaters/flashes, curtain or field loss, double vision, red eye, pain, light sensitivity, headache, nausea, injury, chemical exposure. Sudden vision loss, eye pain with vision change, eye injury, chemical injury, or red eye with major vision change needs urgent evaluation. |
| Urinary and bladder symptoms | Day/night frequency, urgency, leaking, inability to urinate, burning, blood, fever, flank pain, pelvic pain, sleep impact, embarrassment, and quality of life. Blood in urine, fever with back/flank pain, inability to urinate, or severe pain should not be handled with water or supplements alone. |
| Back, joints, and movement | Ability to stand, walk, bear weight, climb stairs, lift, turn, squat; injury, fall, weakness, numbness, radiating pain, bowel/bladder change, fever, cancer history, older age. Serious injury, inability to bear weight, new weakness/numbness, fever, or bowel/bladder symptoms need urgent care. |
| Skin and external products | Clear photos over time, location, itch/pain, drainage/crusting, new skincare, ointments, oral meds, supplements, workplace or plant/chemical exposures. Rapid spreading, fever, severe pain, infection around eyes/face, or immune compromise should be escalated. |
| Dental and oral procedures | Pain, swelling, fever, trauma, bleeding, scheduled extraction/implant/surgery, anticoagulants, antiplatelets, osteoporosis medicines, cancer therapy, immune suppression, steroid use, prior bleeding or poor wound healing. Do not stop blood thinners on your own before dental care. |
| Pregnancy and postpartum | Pregnancy or within one year postpartum changes the threshold. Severe headache, vision changes, fainting, chest pain, trouble breathing, severe belly pain, heavy bleeding, fever, severe nausea/vomiting, severe swelling/pain in one limb, or thoughts of harming self or baby need urgent care. |

Children, older adults, people who are pregnant or postpartum, people with immune suppression, cancer treatment, kidney disease, liver disease, diabetes, or complex medications may need earlier escalation for the same symptom.

## Women's Health Should Not Be Dismissed As "Just How It Is"

Painful periods, heavy bleeding, abnormal bleeding, pelvic pain, pain with sex, discharge changes, infertility concerns, miscarriage, postpartum leaking, pelvic floor symptoms, hot flashes, sleep disruption, and mood changes deserve clearer language than "normal for women."

Record cycle timing, bleeding amount, pain, function impact, pregnancy possibility, postpartum timing, fever, discharge, medications, and whether symptoms affect school, work, sex, sleep, or daily life.

The family role is not to minimize, shame, or control. It is to help the person get heard.

## Local Symptoms Still Need Whole-Person Information

Specialty visits can look local: one tooth, one eye, one joint, one rash. But procedures and prescriptions may depend on whole-person context.

Always mention:

- current diagnoses and recent hospitalizations;
- all medications, including blood thinners, antiplatelet medicines, steroids, immune suppressants, cancer therapy, osteoporosis medicines, supplements, and herbal products;
- allergies and serious reactions;
- pregnancy/postpartum status when relevant;
- prior surgery, bleeding, infection, clotting problems, poor wound healing, or anesthesia issues.

For dental extractions, implants, periodontal surgery, eye procedures, skin procedures, endoscopy, biopsy, or any procedure involving bleeding, infection risk, anesthesia, cutting, sutures, implants, or wound healing, do not hide medications or supplements. Do not stop prescription medication without clinician guidance.

## Four Questions Before Leaving The Visit

Ask:

1. What risk are we trying to rule out?
2. What question does this test or treatment answer?
3. What symptoms should make me come back earlier or go to urgent/emergency care?
4. If this is not better in one week, one month, or one treatment cycle, what is the next step?

If the patient is a child, older adult, pregnant/postpartum, immunocompromised, in cancer treatment, or taking many medications, add:

> Does this person's situation change the follow-up boundary?
