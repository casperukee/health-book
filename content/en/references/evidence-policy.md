# Evidence Policy

This project is not a medical guideline. But when it discusses red flags, screening, chronic-condition markers, mental health crisis, medication boundaries, or family decisions about care, the boundaries must be calibrated against credible public sources.

Evidence priority:

1. Official agencies and clinical guidelines;
2. Professional society guidelines or consensus statements;
3. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses;
4. High-quality randomized controlled trials;
5. Large cohort studies;
6. Expert explanations, courses, and books;
7. Personal experience and examples.

Writing rules:

- High-risk judgments must be supported by authoritative sources.
- Numeric thresholds must identify the source and review date when they are used.
- If guidance may change, write "as of YYYY-MM" or an equivalent review date.
- A single news story or single new study can be a clue, but should not directly rewrite the book's conclusion.
- Uncertain or contested areas should state uncertainty and avoid definitive personal advice.
